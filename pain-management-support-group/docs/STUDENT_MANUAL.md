## WEEK 1: Understanding Your Pain
### Theme: "What is Pain? Biology Meets Experience"

### Week 1 Overview

Pain is not just a physical sensation - it's a complex experience involving your body, mind, and emotions. This week, we'll explore the science of pain in a way that makes sense without needing a medical degree.

**Key Questions This Week:**
- Why do I hurt?
- What's happening in my body when I feel pain?
- Why does my pain sometimes feel worse than the "injury" would suggest?
- Can my brain change the way I experience pain?

### Key Concepts

#### 1. Acute vs. Chronic Pain

**Acute Pain:**
- Short-term (days to weeks)
- Has a clear cause
- Serves as a warning signal ("Stop! You're injured!")
- Usually goes away when the injury heals

**Chronic Pain:**
- Long-term (3+ months)
- May persist after healing
- Doesn't serve a protective purpose anymore
- Affects your whole life (sleep, mood, relationships, work)

**Important**: Chronic pain is real pain. It's not "all in your head" or "just psychological."

#### 2. The Pain Pathway

Think of pain as a message that travels from the site of injury to your brain:

1. **Nociceptors** (pain sensors in your body) detect damage
2. **Nerves** carry the message up your spinal cord
3. **Spinal cord** processes and can amplify or dampen the signal
4. **Brain** receives the message and creates the experience of pain

**Key Insight**: Your brain is the ultimate decision-maker about pain intensity.

#### 3. Types of Pain

**Nociceptive Pain** (tissue damage):
- Stubbing your toe
- Arthritis in your knee
- Muscle strain

**Neuropathic Pain** (nerve damage):
- Burning, shooting, electrical sensations
- Diabetic neuropathy
- Post-shingles pain

**Nociplastic Pain** (sensitization):
- Pain processing system is "turned up"
- Fibromyalgia
- May feel pain from normally non-painful stimuli

**You might have more than one type!**

#### 4. The Nervous System

**Central Nervous System:**
- Brain and spinal cord
- Command center

**Peripheral Nervous System:**
- Nerves throughout your body
- Carries messages to and from the CNS

**Autonomic Nervous System:**
- Automatic functions (heart rate, breathing, digestion)
- Two branches:
  - **Sympathetic**: "Fight or flight" (stress response)
  - **Parasympathetic**: "Rest and digest" (relaxation)

**Chronic pain often triggers the sympathetic system - you're in constant "alert" mode!**

## WEEK 2: Medications and Your Body
### Theme: "Medicine and Your Body: Knowledge is Power"

### Week 2 Overview

Medications can be powerful tools for pain management, but they come with risks. This week, we'll learn how common pain medications work, their side effects, and how to use them more safely and effectively.

**Key Questions This Week:**
- How do my pain medications actually work?
- What are the risks I should know about?
- Are there safer ways to use these medications?
- When should I question my prescriptions?

### Key Concepts

#### 1. How Medications Work in Your Body

**Pharmacokinetics** (fancy word for the medication journey):
1. **Absorption**: Medicine enters your bloodstream
2. **Distribution**: Blood carries it throughout your body
3. **Metabolism**: Liver processes the medication
4. **Excretion**: Kidneys remove it from your body

**Half-Life**: How long the medication stays in your system
- Short half-life: Need to take more frequently
- Long half-life: Stays in system longer, risk of buildup

#### 2. Acetaminophen (Tylenol)

**How it Works:**
- Reduces pain signals in the brain
- Doesn't reduce inflammation (unlike NSAIDs)
- Also reduces fever

**Safety Concerns:**
- **MAXIMUM DOSE**: 3,000-4,000mg per day (don't exceed!)
- Liver damage risk if too much taken
- HIDDEN in many cold/flu medications - check labels!
- Dangerous with alcohol (3+ drinks/day increases liver risk)

**Red Flags - Go to ER:**
- Nausea, vomiting, loss of appetite after taking
- Yellow skin or eyes
- Dark urine
- Confusion

**Your Safety Checklist:**
☐ I know my total daily dose
☐ I've checked all my medications for "hidden" acetaminophen
☐ I limit alcohol when taking acetaminophen
☐ I don't exceed 3,000mg per day

#### 3. NSAIDs (Ibuprofen, Naproxen, etc.)

**Common NSAIDs:**
- Ibuprofen (Advil, Motrin)
- Naproxen (Aleve)
- Aspirin
- Celecoxib (Celebrex) - prescription

**How They Work:**
- Block COX enzymes
- This stops production of prostaglandins (chemicals that cause inflammation and pain)

**Benefits:**
- Reduce inflammation
- Reduce pain
- Reduce fever

**Risks:**
1. **Stomach/GI Issues:**
   - Ulcers
   - Bleeding
   - Higher risk if over 65, history of ulcers, or taking blood thinners

2. **Heart/Cardiovascular:**
   - Increased risk of heart attack and stroke
   - Raises blood pressure
   - Can worsen heart failure

3. **Kidney Problems:**
   - Can damage kidneys, especially if dehydrated
   - Higher risk with high blood pressure or heart failure

**Safer Use Tips:**
- Take with food
- Use lowest effective dose
- Use for shortest time possible
- Stay well-hydrated
- Avoid if you have heart disease (talk to doctor)

**Red Flags - Call Doctor:**
- Black or bloody stools
- Severe stomach pain
- Chest pain
- Sudden swelling (feet, ankles)
- Shortness of breath

#### 4. Opioid Medications

**Common Opioids:**
- Hydrocodone (Vicodin, Norco)
- Oxycodone (Percocet, OxyContin)
- Morphine
- Tramadol
- Codeine

**How They Work:**
- Bind to opioid receptors in brain and spinal cord
- Block pain signals
- Release dopamine (feel-good chemical)

**Your Body Makes Its Own "Opioids":**
- Endorphins
- Enkephalins
- These natural painkillers are released during exercise, laughter, etc.

**Expected Side Effects:**
- Constipation (90% of people - VERY common)
- Drowsiness
- Nausea (usually improves after 1-2 weeks)
- Itching

**Serious Risks:**
1. **Respiratory Depression** (slowed breathing)
   - NEVER mix with alcohol or benzodiazepines (Xanax, Valium)
   - Higher risk at higher doses

2. **Tolerance**
   - Need more medication for same effect
   - Develops over weeks to months

3. **Physical Dependence**
   - Body adapts to medication
   - Withdrawal symptoms if stopped suddenly
   - NOT the same as addiction

4. **Addiction/Opioid Use Disorder**
   - Compulsive use despite harm
   - 3-8% of pain patients develop this
   - Higher risk with personal/family history of substance use

**Constipation Management (ESSENTIAL!):**
- Start stool softener WITH first opioid dose
- Increase fiber and water
- Stay active (movement helps)
- Ask doctor about prescription options if OTC doesn't work

**Naloxone (Narcan):**
- Reverses opioid overdose
- Nasal spray - easy to use
- ASK YOUR DOCTOR FOR A PRESCRIPTION
- Have family/friends know where it is

**Important Truths:**
- Long-term opioids have limited evidence for chronic pain
- They work well for short-term acute pain
- Functional improvement matters more than pain scores
- It's okay to question if they're helping

#### 5. Topical Treatments

**Lidocaine Patches:**
- Numbs the area locally
- Very little absorbed into bloodstream
- Works best for localized nerve pain

**Capsaicin Cream:**
- From hot peppers
- Depletes substance P (pain chemical)
- Burns initially - some people can't tolerate it
- Takes 2-4 weeks to work

**Diclofenac Gel (Voltaren):**
- NSAID absorbed through skin
- Works for surface joints (knee, hand)
- Lower risk than oral NSAIDs but not zero risk
