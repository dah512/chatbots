# Pain Management Support Group - 8-Week Curriculum

## Program Overview
**Duration:** 8 weeks
**Sessions per Week:** 4 sessions
**Session Length:** 2.5 hours each
**Total Sessions:** 32
**Target Audience:** Middle-aged adults with chronic pain from multiple conditions

## Program Goals
1. **Improve sleep quality** and reduce fatigue
2. **Reduce anxiety** and psychological burden of pain
3. **Improve physical function:** strength, range of motion, flexibility
4. **Enhance ability** to complete activities of daily living (ADLs)
5. **Educate on alternatives** to traditional pain management
6. **Empower patients** to advocate for their care
7. **Reduce power differential** between patients and providers

## Session Structure (2.5 hours)
- **0:00-0:20** - Pre-session assessment & check-in
- **0:20-0:40** - Lecture/Education Part 1
- **0:40-0:50** - Q&A and Discussion
- **0:50-1:10** - Lecture/Education Part 2
- **1:10-1:20** - Break
- **1:20-1:50** - Movement Practice (Tai Chi/Qi Gong/Yoga)
- **1:50-2:10** - Nutrition/Lifestyle Discussion
- **2:10-2:25** - Group Discussion & Sharing
- **2:25-2:30** - Post-session assessment & homework

## Top 10 Pain Conditions Addressed
1. Lower back pain (lumbar radiculopathy)
2. Osteoarthritis (knees, hips, hands)
3. Rheumatoid arthritis
4. Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE)
5. Scleroderma
6. Sjögren's syndrome
7. Fibromyalgia
8. Cervical spine disorders (neck pain)
9. Shoulder injuries (rotator cuff, frozen shoulder)
10. Post-surgical pain syndromes

### Session 2.2: Opioids - History, Mechanism, and Reality
**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand opioid mechanism of action
- Learn about endogenous opioid system
- Discuss appropriate opioid use and concerns

**Lecture Topics:**
1. **Endogenous Opioid System:**
   - Endorphins, enkephalins, dynorphins
   - POMC (proopiomelanocortin) and peptide processing
   - Opioid receptors: mu (μ), delta (δ), kappa (κ)

2. **Exogenous Opioids:**
   - Natural opiates (morphine, codeine)
   - Semi-synthetic opioids (oxycodone, hydrocodone)

   - Mechanism: receptor binding and G-protein signaling

### Session 2.3: NSAIDs and OTC Pain Relievers - Benefits and Risks
**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand NSAID mechanism (COX-1 and COX-2 inhibition)
- Learn about acetaminophen hepatotoxicity
- Identify safe usage guidelines and red flags

**Lecture Topics:**
1. **NSAIDs:**
   - Mechanism: Cyclooxygenase (COX) inhibition
   - Prostaglandin synthesis and inflammation
   - Common NSAIDs: ibuprofen, naproxen, aspirin, celecoxib
   - Side effects: GI bleeding, cardiovascular risks, kidney damage

2. **Acetaminophen (Tylenol):**
   - Mechanism: Central COX inhibition (not fully understood)
   - Metabolism in the liver
   - Hepatotoxicity risk: NAPQI formation
   - Maximum daily dose: 3000-4000mg
   - Hidden acetaminophen in combination products

**Visual Aids Needed:**
- COX pathway diagram (arachidonic acid → prostaglandins)
- Acetaminophen metabolism flowchart
- Side effect comparison table
- Warning signs infographic (GI bleeding, liver damage)

**Research Data to Include:**
- AAFP data on NSAID-related hospitalizations
- Acetaminophen overdose statistics
- Long-term NSAID use cardiovascular risk data

**Movement Practice:** Tai Chi for reducing inflammation naturally
**Nutrition Topic:** Alternatives to NSAIDs (turmeric, ginger)
**Assessment:** OTC medication safety quiz

### Session 2.4: Adjuvant Medications and Topical Treatments
**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand how antidepressants and anticonvulsants treat pain
- Learn about topical treatments beyond capsaicin
- Discuss compound creams and personalized formulations

**Lecture Topics:**
1. **Adjuvant Medications:**
   - Antidepressants: TCAs (amitriptyline), SNRIs (duloxetine)
   - Mechanism: Serotonin/norepinephrine modulation of pain
   - Anticonvulsants: gabapentin, pregabalin
   - Mechanism: Voltage-gated calcium channel modulation
   - Muscle relaxants: mechanism and appropriate use

2. **Topical Treatments:**
   - Lidocaine patches: local anesthetic mechanism
   - Capsaicin: TRPV1 receptor desensitization
   - Diclofenac gel: topical NSAID
   - Compound creams: ketamine, baclofen, combinations
   - Essential oils: Evidence vs. anecdote

**Visual Aids Needed:**
- Neurotransmitter pathway diagram
- Calcium channel structure
- Skin penetration diagram for topicals
- Comparison chart: topical treatments effectiveness

**Movement Practice:** Qi Gong for neuropathic pain
**Nutrition Topic:** Magnesium and muscle relaxation
**Assessment:** Week 2 comprehensive post-test

# Assessment Strategy

## Weekly Pre-Tests
- Administered at start of Week's first session
- 10-15 multiple choice questions
- Assess baseline knowledge of week's topics
- Identify areas needing extra attention

## Weekly Post-Tests
- Administered at end of Week's last session
- 10-15 multiple choice questions (different from pre-test)
- Measure knowledge gains
- Include practical application questions

## Functional Assessments (Ongoing)
- **Week 1:** Baseline pain levels (VAS 0-10), GAD-7, sleep quality
- **Week 2-3:** Range of motion measurements, ADL assessment
- **Week 4-5:** Pain catastrophizing scale, quality of life measures
- **Week 6-7:** Physical function tests (timed up and go, sit to stand)
- **Week 8:** Repeat all baseline measures, satisfaction survey

## Outcomes Measured
1. Pain intensity (daily logs, weekly averages)
2. Sleep quality (Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index)
3. Fatigue (FACIT-Fatigue scale)
4. Anxiety (GAD-7)
5. Depression (PHQ-9)
6. Physical function (objective tests + self-report)
7. Self-efficacy (Pain Self-Efficacy Questionnaire)
8. Medication usage changes
9. Healthcare utilization
10. Knowledge gain (pre/post test scores)

# Conclusion

This 8-week curriculum provides comprehensive education, empowerment, and practical skills for adults living with chronic pain from diverse conditions. By addressing biological, psychological, and social aspects of pain, and by reducing power imbalances in healthcare, participants gain knowledge and confidence to better manage their pain and improve their quality of life.

The program emphasizes evidence-based information while remaining accessible to those without medical backgrounds. The integration of movement, nutrition, and mind-body practices alongside traditional medical education creates a truly holistic approach to pain management.

Most importantly, by creating a safe space for open discussion without fear of reprisal, this support group fills a critical gap in the pain care landscape.
