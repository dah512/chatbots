## Acetaminophen (Tylenol)

### Overview
- **Mechanism**: Inhibits COX enzymes in the CNS (exact mechanism still debated)
- **Common Uses**: Mild-to-moderate pain, fever reduction
- **Maximum Daily Dose**: 3,000-4,000mg for adults (FDA lowered recommendation to 3,000mg in 2011)

### Hepatotoxicity Statistics

#### Acute Liver Failure (ALF)
- **Leading Cause**: Acetaminophen is the #1 cause of acute liver failure in the United States
- **Annual Cases**: ~500 deaths and 56,000 ER visits annually related to acetaminophen overdose (CDC 2020)
- **Unintentional Overdose**: 48% of acetaminophen-related ALF cases are unintentional (AAFP, 2015)

#### Risk Factors for Toxicity
| Risk Factor | Increased Risk | Notes |
|-------------|----------------|-------|
| Chronic alcohol use | 3-4x | Induces CYP2E1, depletes glutathione |
| Fasting/malnutrition | 2-3x | Reduced glutathione stores |
| Concurrent medications | Variable | CYP450 inducers increase risk |
| Chronic dosing at upper limits | 1.5-2x | 4g/day for > 3-4 days |

#### Key Statistics (AAFP Data 2004-2024)
- **ER Visits**: 78,000 annual ER visits for acetaminophen-related issues
- **Hospitalizations**: 33,000 hospitalizations per year
- **Therapeutic Misadventure**: 25% of cases involve taking multiple products containing acetaminophen unknowingly
- **Hidden Acetaminophen**: Found in >600 OTC and prescription products

### FDA Actions
- **2011**: Prescription products limited to 325mg per dosing unit
- **2014**: Boxed warning added to all acetaminophen prescriptions
- **2017**: New labeling requirements for OTC products highlighting liver risk

### Clinical Pearls
- **NAPQI Formation**: Toxic metabolite forms when glutathione depleted
- **Treatment Window**: N-acetylcysteine (NAC) most effective within 8 hours
- **Chronic Risk**: Even "therapeutic" doses (3-4g daily) for weeks can cause liver injury in susceptible individuals

## NSAIDs (Ibuprofen, Naproxen, Others)

### Overview
- **Mechanism**: Inhibit cyclooxygenase (COX-1 and COX-2) enzymes
- **Common Drugs**:
  - Ibuprofen (Advil, Motrin): 200-800mg doses, max 3200mg/day
  - Naproxen (Aleve): 220-500mg doses, max 1500mg/day
  - Celecoxib (Celebrex): Selective COX-2 inhibitor
  - Aspirin: Irreversible COX inhibitor

### Gastrointestinal Complications

#### Upper GI Bleeding
- **Incidence**: 1-2% per year in long-term NSAID users
- **Hospitalization**: ~100,000 hospitalizations annually in US
- **Mortality**: 7,000-10,000 deaths per year from NSAID-related GI complications

#### Risk Stratification (AAFP Guidelines 2018)
| Risk Level | Annual Bleed Risk | Risk Factors |
|------------|------------------|--------------|
| Low | <1% | Age <65, no history |
| Moderate | 1-2% | Age >65 OR history of uncomplicated ulcer |
| High | 2-4% | Age >65 + history, or multiple risk factors |
| Very High | >4% | Prior bleeding + ongoing NSAID use |

#### Risk Factors for GI Bleeding
1. **Age >65**: 4-5x increased risk
2. **Prior peptic ulcer disease**: 6-7x risk
3. **Concurrent corticosteroids**: 4x risk
4. **Concurrent anticoagulants**: 12-15x risk
5. **Concurrent SSRIs**: 2-3x risk
6. **H. pylori infection**: 1.5-2x risk
7. **High-dose NSAID use**: 2-3x risk

### Cardiovascular Complications

#### Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses
Based on analysis of 639,000+ participants (NEJM 2016, Lancet 2013):

| NSAID | CV Event Risk | Notes |
|-------|---------------|-------|
| Diclofenac | +40% | Highest CV risk |
| Ibuprofen (>1200mg/day) | +20-30% | Dose-dependent |
| Naproxen | Neutral to +10% | Lowest CV risk among NSAIDs |
| Celecoxib | +15-20% | Lower than thought |
| All NSAIDs | +20-50% | Varies by drug and dose |

#### Key Findings (FDA Black Box Warning 2015)
- **MI Risk**: Increases within first week of use
- **Stroke Risk**: Elevated with all NSAIDs
- **Heart Failure**: 19% increased risk of hospitalization
- **Duration**: Risk increases with longer duration
- **Dose**: Higher doses = higher risk

### Renal Complications

#### Acute Kidney Injury (AKI)
- **Mechanism**: Inhibition of prostaglandin-mediated renal vasodilation
- **Incidence**: 1-5% in regular users
- **Risk Factors**: Dehydration, CHF, cirrhosis, chronic kidney disease, ACE inhibitors

#### Chronic Kidney Disease
- **Regular Use**: 2-3x increased risk of chronic kidney disease
- **Analgesic Nephropathy**: Now rare but historically significant
- **Interstitial Nephritis**: Occurs in <1% but can be severe

### Other NSAID Complications
- **Hypertension**: 5-10 mmHg increase in BP with regular use
- **Asthma Exacerbation**: 10-20% of asthmatics have aspirin sensitivity
- **Rash/Hypersensitivity**: 1-3% incidence
- **Liver Injury**: Rare (<1% per year) but can be severe

### COX-2 Selective Inhibitors
**Celecoxib (Celebrex):**
- Lower GI bleeding risk vs non-selective NSAIDs (0.8% vs 1.4% annually)
- Similar CV risk to ibuprofen in recent trials
- No clear renal advantage

**Rofecoxib (Vioxx) - WITHDRAWN 2004:**
- 2x increased MI risk
- Led to market withdrawal and FDA class-wide warnings

## Opioid Analgesics

### Current Opioid Crisis Statistics

#### Overdose Deaths (CDC Data)
- **1999**: 8,050 opioid overdose deaths
- **2010**: 21,089 deaths
- **2020**: 68,630 deaths
- **2021**: 80,411 deaths (record high)
- **2022**: 81,806 deaths

#### Prescription Opioids vs Illicit
- **Prescription Opioids**: ~15,000 deaths annually
- **Heroin**: ~15,000 deaths annually
- **Synthetic opioids (fentanyl)**: ~70,000 deaths annually (2022)

### Prescription Opioid Use Patterns

#### Prescribing Trends
- **Peak (2012)**: 255 million opioid prescriptions
- **2021**: 142 million prescriptions (45% reduction)
- **High-Dose Prescriptions**: Decreased from 11.5% to 4.7% (2008-2020)
- **Long-Term Use**: 3-4% of US adults on chronic opioid therapy

### Adverse Effects of Opioids

#### Common Side Effects (Incidence)
| Side Effect | Incidence | Management |
|-------------|-----------|------------|
| Constipation | 40-90% | Aggressive bowel regimen essential |
| Nausea/Vomiting | 10-30% | Often improves after 1-2 weeks |
| Sedation | 20-60% | Dose reduction, tolerance develops |
| Pruritus (itching) | 2-10% | Antihistamines, rotate opioid |
| Respiratory Depression | <1% (therapeutic doses) | Higher with benzodiazepines |

#### Serious Complications
1. **Opioid-Induced Hyperalgesia (OIH)**
   - Incidence: 5-10% of long-term users
   - Paradoxical increase in pain sensitivity
   - Management: Opioid rotation or tapering

2. **Hypogonadism**
   - Incidence: 50-75% of long-term male users, 25-50% females
   - Low testosterone, low libido, osteoporosis
   - Often unrecognized and undertreated

3. **Immunosuppression**
   - Opioids suppress immune function
   - Increased infection risk (unclear clinical significance)

4. **Sleep-Disordered Breathing**
   - Central sleep apnea in 20-30% of chronic users
   - Increased risk at higher doses (>200 MME/day)

5. **Falls and Fractures**
   - 2-3x increased fall risk in elderly
   - Hip fracture risk increased 2.4x

### Opioid Dependence and Addiction

#### Risk Statistics
- **Iatrogenic Addiction**: 3-8% of opioid-naive patients prescribed opioids for chronic pain develop opioid use disorder (OUD)
- **Risk Factors**: Personal/family history of substance use disorder, mental health disorders, younger age
- **Transition to Heroin**: 4-6% of prescription opioid misusers transition to heroin

#### Withdrawal Syndrome
- Onset: 6-12 hours (short-acting), 30-48 hours (long-acting)
- Duration: 7-10 days
- Symptoms: Anxiety, muscle aches, insomnia, GI distress
- Mortality: Rare from withdrawal alone (unlike alcohol/benzos)

### CDC Opioid Prescribing Guidelines (2016, Updated 2022)

#### Key Recommendations
1. Non-opioid therapy preferred for chronic pain
2. Start with lowest effective dose
3. Prescribe ≤3-7 days for acute pain
4. Avoid concurrent benzodiazepines
5. Check prescription drug monitoring program (PDMP)
6. Urine drug testing for chronic therapy
7. Offer naloxone for overdose reversal

#### Morphine Milligram Equivalent (MME) Thresholds
- **50 MME/day**: Increased caution and monitoring
- **90 MME/day**: Carefully justify or taper
- **200+ MME/day**: 9x increased overdose risk vs <20 MME

### Efficacy Questions
- **Chronic Non-Cancer Pain**: Weak evidence for long-term benefit
- **Number Needed to Treat (NNT)**: 4-10 for short-term pain relief
- **Functional Improvement**: Limited evidence beyond 3-6 months
- **Quality of Life**: Mixed results in long-term studies

## Topical Treatments

### Lidocaine Patches

#### Efficacy
- **Neuropathic Pain**: NNT = 4.4 for 50% pain reduction (post-herpetic neuralgia)
- **Other Pain**: Limited evidence for non-neuropathic pain
- **Mechanism**: Local sodium channel blockade

#### Safety
- **Systemic Absorption**: Minimal (<3% absorbed)
- **Side Effects**: Skin reactions (4-8%), rare systemic effects
- **Contraindications**: Large area application (>3 patches), broken skin

### Capsaicin Cream/Patches

#### Efficacy
- **Neuropathic Pain**: NNT = 8-10 for 50% pain reduction
- **OA Pain**: NNT = 10-12 (modest benefit)
- **High-Concentration Patch (8%)**: Better efficacy, requires provider application

#### Patient Experience Issues
- **Initial Burning**: 40-70% experience significant burning
- **Adherence**: 25-35% discontinue due to irritation
- **Time to Benefit**: 2-4 weeks for full effect
- **Requires Multiple Daily Applications**: Compliance challenges

### Diclofenac Gel (Voltaren Gel)

#### Efficacy
- **Osteoarthritis**: NNT = 6-9 for 50% pain reduction
- **Equivalent to Oral**: Similar efficacy to oral NSAIDs for localized OA

#### Safety
- **Systemic Absorption**: 6-10% of oral exposure
- **GI Safety**: Better GI safety profile than oral NSAIDs
- **CV Risk**: Still present but lower than oral NSAIDs
- **Limitation**: Only effective for superficial joints (knee, hand)

### Compounded Topical Creams

#### Common Formulations
- Ketamine 10% + Baclofen 2% + Cyclobenzaprine 2%
- Ketamine + Gabapentin + various others
- Diclofenac + various adjuvants

#### Evidence Issues
- **Efficacy**: Limited high-quality evidence
- **Variability**: Quality varies by compounding pharmacy
- **Cost**: Often expensive, insurance may not cover
- **FDA Regulation**: Less stringent than commercial products

## Comparative Risk Analysis

### Mortality Risk (Annual Deaths per 100,000 Users)

| Medication Class | Deaths per 100K Users | Primary Causes |
|------------------|----------------------|----------------|
| Opioids | 15-20 | Overdose, respiratory depression |
| NSAIDs | 3-5 | GI bleeding, cardiovascular events |
| Acetaminophen | 0.4-0.8 | Acute liver failure |
| Topicals | <0.01 | Extremely rare systemic effects |

### Hospitalization Risk (Annual Rate per 1,000 Users)

| Medication Class | Hospitalizations per 1,000 | Common Causes |
|------------------|---------------------------|---------------|
| NSAIDs | 10-20 | GI bleeding, AKI, CV events |
| Opioids | 5-15 | Overdose, falls, complications |
| Acetaminophen | 1-2 | Liver injury |
| Topicals | <1 | Skin reactions |

### Number Needed to Harm (NNH) - One Year of Treatment

| Medication | Serious Adverse Event | NNH |
|------------|----------------------|-----|
| NSAIDs (chronic use) | GI bleed requiring hospitalization | 150-200 |
| NSAIDs (chronic use) | MI or stroke | 500-1000 |
| Opioids (chronic use) | Fall with injury | 50-100 |
| Opioids (chronic use) | Opioid use disorder | 15-30 |
| Acetaminophen (chronic, max dose) | Liver enzyme elevation | 100-200 |
| Acetaminophen (overdose) | Acute liver failure | 20-50 (overdose cohort) |

### Benefit-Risk Assessment

#### For Acute Pain (<7 days)
**Most Favorable Risk Profile:**
1. Acetaminophen (appropriate dosing)
2. NSAIDs (short-term, low-dose)
3. Opioids (3-7 days max, low-dose)
4. Topicals (limited efficacy)

#### For Chronic Pain (>3 months)
**Most Favorable Risk Profile:**
1. Topicals (limited efficacy, excellent safety)
2. Acetaminophen (lower than previously thought efficacy)
3. NSAIDs (significant risks, careful patient selection)
4. Opioids (highest risk, limited long-term efficacy)

## Key Takeaways for Patient Education

### Acetaminophen Safety
1. **Never exceed 3,000mg per day** without physician supervision
2. **Check all medications** for hidden acetaminophen (cold/flu products)
3. **Avoid alcohol** or limit to 1 drink/day while taking acetaminophen
4. **Seek immediate care** for overdose (>4g single dose or >6g/day)

### NSAID Safety
1. **Take with food** to reduce GI irritation
2. **Use lowest effective dose** for shortest time possible
3. **Monitor blood pressure** - NSAIDs can elevate BP
4. **Risk increases with age** - especially over 65
5. **Avoid if on blood thinners** or with history of ulcers
6. **Stay hydrated** - NSAIDs can affect kidneys

### Opioid Safety
1. **Never combine with alcohol or benzodiazepines**
2. **Use exactly as prescribed**
3. **Store securely** and dispose of unused medication properly
4. **Obtain naloxone** (Narcan) for overdose reversal
5. **Bowel regimen required** - start with first dose
6. **Long-term use** rarely appropriate for chronic non-cancer pain

### When to Seek Medical Care
- **Acetaminophen**: Accidental overdose, even if feeling okay
- **NSAIDs**: Black/tarry stools, severe stomach pain, chest pain, sudden swelling
- **Opioids**: Extreme drowsiness, confusion, slow breathing, unable to wake
